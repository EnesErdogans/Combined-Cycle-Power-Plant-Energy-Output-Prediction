1. Problem Definition

A Combined Cycle Power Plant (CCPP) integrates gas turbines (GT) and steam turbines (ST) connected through heat recovery steam generators (HRSG). In this configuration, the exhaust heat from the gas turbine is used to generate steam, which drives a secondary steam turbine. This "combined cycle" achieves thermal efficiencies of up to 60%, significantly higher than simple-cycle plants (~35%).

The net electrical energy output of a CCPP is strongly influenced by ambient conditions. Gas turbine performance degrades at higher ambient temperatures (lower air density reduces mass flow rate), while steam turbine efficiency is affected by exhaust vacuum levels and humidity. Understanding and predicting this relationship is critical for power grid load planning and plant performance optimization.

🎯 Your Objective

Build an ANN model to predict the net hourly electrical energy output (MW) of a combined cycle power plant based on four ambient variables. The dataset contains 9,568 hourly measurements collected over 6 years (2006–2011) when the plant operated at full load.

The data was collected from sensors distributed throughout the plant, recording ambient conditions every second. The values provided are hourly averages.
